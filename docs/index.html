RECOVERY LAB

A personal health agent that reasons over your own data, remembers what you tell it, and tests what actually works

Every morning, as soon as WHOOP scores the night, an agent reads Python-computed evidence, its memory of you and its own notes from yesterday, calls tools when it needs more, and decides on one action, or none. Code checks every claim; general health facts come from a cited knowledge base. A coach chat and randomized N-of-1 experiments close the loop, and every design choice is backed by an evaluation.

2. The daily agent: observe, reason, act, learn

The model never sees raw records. Every fact it can use is a numbered evidence item, and every number in its answer is checked against them; if a check fails it gets one revision, then the transparent rule engine's plan is used.

3. The coach: ask it anytime

The email links to a chat with the same tools plus three more: free time, saving what the user says (goals, preferences, corrections) to memory, and proposing a calendar block. Proposals are checked in code and written only after a tap. A real exchange from this snapshot:

I want to fall asleep earlier. What should I change this week?
Your usual bedtime over the last 14 nights is 01:39, and last night you fell asleep at 00:16. That's your earliest bedtime in this stretch apart from 23:59 on Oct 7, so you've already shown you can do it. Step 1: aim for lights out around 00:15 tonight, then move it about 15 minutes earlier every couple of nights toward your midnight target. Step 2: tonight is a comparison night in your screen experiment, so keep your usual evening and screens are fine. I won't add a screen-free wind-down block tonight. You have nothing on the calendar, so the timing is easy to fit. Would you like me to put a lights-out reminder on tomorrow's calendar once tonight's result is in?
tools it chose: get_today, get_memory, get_metric_history, get_free_time

4. Measured, not eyeballed

Design choices on this page come from offline evaluations that anyone can rerun (make eval, make eval-retrieval).

Knowledge retrieval (RAG)

General health facts come from 18 curated passages, each paraphrased from a named source and cited in the reply. Which search to use was decided on 30 labeled questions, several worded without the passages' vocabulary:

SearchHit@1Hit@3MRR
Keyword (BM25)57%90%0.72
Dense (voyage-4-lite)97%100%0.98
Hybrid (RRF)83%97%0.90

Hit@k: the right passage is among the top k. Keyword search missed paraphrases ("Instagram" for screens), and equal-weight fusion let those misses pull dense hits down, so the agents use dense search.

5. Experiments: does a habit actually help me?

The AI may propose an experiment from a fixed list when the data makes it relevant; randomization, adherence and statistics stay in code. The verdict is pre-specified: helped, worse, no meaningful effect, or inconclusive.

Running experiment · night 4 of 14

No screens 30 minutes before bed

Do screen-free last 30 minutes before bed improve your sleep?

Nights come in pairs; a seeded coin flip decides which night of each pair is the habit night. Adherence comes from a one-tap Yes/No the next morning, never from the outcome. Future assignments stay hidden, and there are no interim results: peeking and stopping early would inflate false positives.
